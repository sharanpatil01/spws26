Flight & rockets · Machines story 03

From wings to reusable rockets

Aircraft and drones need air. Rockets carry their own oxygen, allowing them to climb where the atmosphere becomes thin and then reach space.

A drone pushes air downward and the air pushes back upward. Adding propellers gives it balance and direction. An airplane uses wings and forward speed: as air moves over its wings, pressure differences create lift.

Spacecraft think differently

A rocket works by throwing hot gas backward. Newton's third law explains the push: action creates an equal and opposite reaction. ISRO, NASA, and SpaceX use different rocket designs, but they share the same challenges—fuel, weight, heat, weather, guidance, and safety.

Reusable flight

SpaceX became famous for recovering boosters and preparing them for another flight. Reuse aims to lower the cost and work involved in launches. It does not erase the danger: every launch still needs inspection, maintenance, testing, and a strong safety culture.

The big idea

Different machines solve different parts of flight. Drones hover locally, airplanes carry people efficiently through air, and rockets escape Earth's atmosphere. Curiosity connects them all.
