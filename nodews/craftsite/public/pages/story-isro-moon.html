ISRO takes India to the Moon

From Chandrayaan to a precision landing near the south pole, India's space journey shows how patience, teamwork, and careful data make bold missions possible.

The Indian Space Research Organisation, or ISRO, builds missions with highly skilled teams across the country. Chandrayaan-1 helped map signs of water molecules on the Moon. Chandrayaan-2 tested an advanced landing system, while Chandrayaan-3 completed India's successful soft landing in 2023.

Why the Moon's south pole?

The region receives light and long darkness in patterns very different from the Moon's equator. Permanently shadowed craters may preserve water ice. Scientists want to understand whether that ice is useful to future explorers and what clues it holds about lunar history.

Try a mission design

A mission begins with a question, not a rocket. Engineers choose instruments, calculate fuel, plan communication delays, and build a way to recover safely from possible failures. Success is teamwork between researchers, technicians, pilots, coders, scientists, and decision-makers.

The big idea

ISRO's journey is a story about doing large things with careful steps. Progress is not always spectacular; sometimes it is thousands of quiet decisions made correctly.
