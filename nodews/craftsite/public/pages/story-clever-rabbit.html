Panchatantra · Courage

The Clever
Rabbit

When a powerful herd threatens a rabbit village, one small messenger proves that wisdom can outrun strength.

For twelve dry years, the forest had grown silent. Even ponds and lakes lost their water. When the clouds finally returned, the elephant king found a beautiful lake. But a large herd of elephants stepped into the lake, crushing thousands of rabbit homes on the bank.

The rabbits gathered to decide what to do. Their leader sent a wise rabbit named Victory to speak with the elephant king.

Victory reached the lake at night. The elephant king could not see him from afar and called, “Who are you?”

“I am Victory, messenger of the Moon,” the rabbit answered. “The lake belongs to the Moon. You have disturbed a sacred place, and its reflection has grown angry with you.”

The curious elephants followed him to the water. In the moonlight, Victory pointed at the Moon's reflection dancing between the ripples. “Here is the Moon's messenger,” he said. “Come closer and ask forgiveness.”

The elephant king lowered his trunk into the water. The reflection trembled and vanished beneath the moving surface. Victory told the king that this disturbance proved the Moon had been deeply offended.

The proud king bowed his head in fear. Then the rabbits begged him to leave the lake and find another place to drink. The elephants agreed, and the rabbit village was safe.

Victory had no magic and no great strength. He had only noticed his neighbors' danger and used patience, observation, and courage to solve it.

Lesson

Size and power are not the only kinds of strength. Patient thinking and brave words can protect others.

Adapted from the public-domain Arthur W. Ryder translation, The Panchatantra.
