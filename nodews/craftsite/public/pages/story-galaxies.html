Stars & planets · Space story 03

Beyond the Milky Way

A galaxy is not one star. It is a vast city of stars, planets, gas, dust, gravity, and dark matter moving through the Universe together.

Our Solar System lies inside the Milky Way, a barred spiral galaxy about 100,000 light-years across. Most stars we see are in our own galaxy. Beyond it are billions more galaxies—spirals, smooth ellipticals, and irregular clusters shaped by collisions and gravity.

Looking backward

Light takes time to travel. When a telescope sees a galaxy millions of light-years away, it is seeing the galaxy as it looked millions of years ago. In a sense, astronomy is time travel using telescopes instead of machines.

The Local Group

The Milky Way belongs to a nearby family of galaxies called the Local Group. Its largest member is the Andromeda Galaxy. Astronomers expect the Milky Way and Andromeda will gradually merge in the far future.

The big idea

The Universe is enormous, but we are not lost in it. Measurements, questions, and pictures from observatories allow us to map places we may never visit.
