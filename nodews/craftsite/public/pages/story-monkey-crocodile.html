Panchatantra · Friendship

The Monkey and
the Crocodile

A new friendship brings an unexpected danger—and quick thinking opens a safe path home.

On the shore of a wide sea stood a rose-apple tree, heavy with sweet fruit. A clever monkey named Red-Face lived in its branches. One morning, a crocodile named Ugly-Mug crawled onto the shore and rested beneath the tree.

Red-Face welcomed his visitor. He dropped ripe rose-apples into the crocodile's waiting mouth. In this way, the new friends met every day. They shared fruit, rested in the shade, and talked about the world beyond the sea.

When Ugly-Mug returned home, he carried a few apples for his wife. She tasted them and wondered aloud, “Where have you found fruit that tastes like nectar?”

Ugly-Mug described his friend Red-Face, and the crooked-hearted crocodile wife grew greedy. She decided that if the fruit had made her husband strong, the monkey's heart must be even sweeter.

“Bring him here,” she ordered.

The crocodile was troubled. Red-Face had never harmed him. But his wife's demands were harsh. At last he invited the monkey to visit his home across the sea. He promised that his wife was eager to thank the generous visitor.

Red-Face climbed onto the crocodile's back, and together they crossed the shining water. Then Ugly-Mug confessed what his wife intended.

For a moment, Red-Face was afraid. Then he said calmly, “My heart is not here. I kept it safe in the rose-apple tree. If you turn back, I will bring it to you.”

The crocodile believed the story. He turned toward the shore. At once, Red-Face sprang onto a low branch and climbed out of reach.

Ugly-Mug apologized, but the clever monkey said, “A creature who hides a wicked plan cannot become a true friend. Only trust makes friendship safe.”

Lesson

Think calmly before acting. The quickest solution is not always the answer—clear thinking can save the day.

Adapted from the public-domain Arthur W. Ryder translation, The Panchatantra.
