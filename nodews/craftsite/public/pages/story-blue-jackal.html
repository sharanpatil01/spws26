Panchatantra · Friendship

The Blue
Jackal

A jackal falls into a blue dye, attracts the wrong company, then learns that true friends never need us to pretend.

A young jackal wandered away from his forest home in search of an easy life. At the edge of a village, he slipped into a vat of blue dye. When he climbed out, his brown fur had turned an extraordinary color. The other animals did not recognize him.

A crowd of village dogs and jackals gathered around. They were eager to look important beside someone different. “You are a royal visitor from the distant sky,” they declared. “Rule over us.” They fed him the finest scraps, praised every decision, and laughed at anyone who asked questions.

The blue jackal enjoyed being admired, but he soon grew uneasy around the company he had chosen. His so-called friends only cared about his strange appearance and the benefits he could provide. When they felt hungry, they became demanding. When he made a mistake, they blamed him loudly.

Then one evening, the blue jackal heard the clear howl of a familiar jackal from the forest. He answered without thinking. His real voice revealed him.

The crowd scattered. Ashamed, the blue jackal washed in a river until the dye faded. He went back to the friends who had known him before the village. They welcomed him again, but this time he listened before offering trust. He chose companions who encouraged kindness, told him the truth, and respected him without asking him to become someone else.

Lesson

People who flatter you for your looks, popularity, or usefulness may not be safe friends. Keep distance from those who pressure you to pretend. Build friendships with people who tell the truth, share fairly, and let you remain yourself.

Adapted from the Panchatantra tale of the Blue Jackal; written for young readers.
