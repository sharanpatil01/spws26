Flight & rockets · Mission story 02

NASA explores the red planet

Rovers search Mars for ancient water, past environments, and clues that could show whether life once existed there.

Mars is a cold desert world with a thin atmosphere. NASA's robotic explorers have orbited it, landed on it, flown a small helicopter above it, and driven rovers across its rocks. Each mission answers a few questions and creates new ones.

PerseveranceNASA Mars rover

Seven minutes of careful anxiety

Communicating with Mars takes many minutes, so a rover cannot be controlled live. On landing day, teams on Earth follow instructions already delivered to the spacecraft. Heat shields, parachutes, retro-rockets, and computer decisions must work in sequence.

The road back with samples

Perseverance collects rock samples. Future missions aim to bring selected tubes back to Earth so laboratories can study them with tools that are far more powerful than instruments carried in space. A tiny rock might change the story of life in the Solar System.

The big idea

Robotic missions are team members in exploration. They can endure where humans cannot yet stay, sending evidence back for scientists all over the world to examine.
