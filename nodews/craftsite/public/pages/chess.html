The royal game's story

A journey across centuries

Chess grew from older strategy games played in India about 1,500 years ago. It traveled through Persia and the Arab world, then spread across Europe. Modern rules became more settled in the 1500s. Today, chess is played around the world by millions of children and adults.

A game shaped by many cultures and centuries.

Three beginner rules

Don't rush.
Look before you leap.

  1. ProtectKeep your king safe and guard your valuable pieces.
  2. DevelopMove each knight and bishop into useful squares early.
  3. PlanAsk, “Why is this move useful?” instead of moving at random.
01

First move lesson

Meet the 16-piece team

Set up the board with a light square at the lower-right corner. Each side begins with one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights, and eight pawns.

Remember: “White on the right, queen on her color.”
02

Piece safety

Count before you capture

A move is like trading playground cards. If an opponent can take your piece next, check whether another piece defends it. Never capture a bait piece if it costs a stronger one.

Pause and ask: “What can my opponent play after this?”
03

Smart development

Wake every piece

Do not move the same piece five times at the start. Develop knights and bishops toward the center, protect the king with castling, and open a path for your rooks.

Castle early. Keep your king behind friendly pawns.
04

Checkmate detective

Make a simple plan

Look for checks first. Then choose the check that limits escape squares. Two common beginner mates are a rook-and-king mate and the queen-and-king mate. Practice one puzzle at a time.

Move → check → move checkmated pieces cannot escape.

Your first challenge

Play a slow game against a friend, a parent, or a computer. Say your plan aloud before moving. After the game, name one move that helped and one move that gave away a piece.
