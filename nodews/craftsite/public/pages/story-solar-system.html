Stars & planets · Space story 01

Our remarkable Solar System

A tour from rocky Mercury to distant Neptune, and a reminder that every world has a different way of moving, growing, and shining.

Imagine the Sun at the center of a gigantic classroom. Eight planets circle it in nearly circular paths called orbits. The inner planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—are small rocky worlds. Farther away, Jupiter and Saturn are enormous gas giants, while Uranus and Neptune are cold giants rich in ice.

The neighborhood has surprises

Asteroids crowd a belt between Mars and Jupiter. Comets arrive with bright tails as they warm near the Sun. Moons orbit planets—Jupiter has many, Saturn wears spectacular rings, and Earth has one Moon that helps create tides.

Explorer's notebook

Traveling outward shows a simple rule of nature: distance changes conditions. Closer worlds receive more heat; outer worlds become colder and darker. But space is not empty. It carries dust, radiation, magnetic fields, and answers waiting to be measured.

The big idea

Earth is not only a planet. It is a moving laboratory with liquid water, a protective atmosphere, and life. Protecting it is the first mission every astronaut shares.
